# Cue-led miniature: let phrases and actions set the clock

## Inputs
A cleared music or speech track, or an original sound plan; intended message; optional lyric permissions; duration/fps; two visual motifs; a cue sheet with `time, event, visual action, sound peak, hold, source`. Choose whether speech, music or visible physical action is the timing authority.

## Direction
Use changes in energy to structure attention. A quiet phrase can deserve a still image; a musical accent can motivate a transition. Example: two paper pieces approach on a phrase, touch on one audible note, then separate to reveal a sentence. For a music-machine variation, every note corresponds to a visible contact rather than unrelated decorative bouncing.

## Storyboard and timing
For a twelve-second sketch: 0-3 s introduce motif A with space to listen; 3-6 s answer with motif B; 6-9 s combine them at the main accent; 9-12 s let the phrase resolve and hold. Replace these divisions with actual recorded phrase landmarks. Do not force narration to constant BPM.

## Motion and continuity
Map only structural changes to major cues. Minor motion may bridge beats without adding a cut. For visible impacts, compute a single event time used by both collision pose and audio onset/peak. For lyrics, reveal intelligible phrase groups and use manually checked vocal timestamps. A bass transient detector is not a lyric aligner. Keep the same visual motif recognizable through energetic sections.

## Assets and rights
Use original synthesis or cleared recordings. A music license may not cover lyric display, broadcast, client redistribution or isolated track delivery. Voice use needs authorization; do not clone a person's voice without consent. Keep a license record for each sample and sound effect, not just the music bed.

## Output
Cue sheet, editable scene, original or cleared audio stems where redistribution permits, transcript/captions and mixed MP4. Include a silent version when useful. Record the actual mix and synchronization checks, not a generic loudness claim.

## Pitfalls
One cut every beat; effects masking narration; visual hits aligned to file starts rather than transients; mistaken downbeat; invented song credits; flashing used as rhythm; physical contacts that miss their sound.

## Validation
Listen and watch the complete encoded result. Check opening, middle and ending cues for drift. Verify captions against vocals, cue alignment within the intended frame tolerance, peak levels and no clipping. Confirm the story also works without audio where the brief requires it. Use [audio cues](../techniques/audio-cues.md) for formulas and mix checks.
