# Generative video and hybrid compositing

## Distinguish the routes

- **Generative video:** a model synthesizes moving imagery from text, images or other conditioning. Exact typography, physics, identity and requested frame timing are not guaranteed.
- **Code animation:** a program authored with or without AI renders explicit geometry and text. A prompt to write JavaScript is not a video-model invocation.
- **Hybrid:** recorded or generated plates enter a controlled edit with exact text, masks, camera moves, captions and audio. Generated stills animated in Canvas are a hybrid even without a video generator.

Prefer actual capture for product evidence. A realistic generated interface or action cannot prove a feature works.

## Before spending or uploading

Obtain approval for the provider, model, input uploads, intended use, budget ceiling and retry count. Verify current input/output capabilities, duration, aspect ratio, audio behavior, output rights, privacy/retention and commercial restrictions from the actual provider. Do not inherit credentials, spend authorizations or private paths embedded in a source prompt. Never assume a model alias from a gallery still exists or has the claimed capability.

## Plan a bounded shot

Use this original shot record:

- **Purpose:** the visible change the shot must communicate.
- **Start/end:** subject, position, pose, background and state that must connect to neighboring shots.
- **Spatial map:** camera side, eyeline, travel direction, foreground/background and occlusion.
- **Performance:** one principal action, its pace and meaningful pause.
- **Camera/light:** one camera intention and a coherent light source.
- **Constraints:** essential identity features and prohibited defects, kept short enough to review.
- **Assets:** references with owner, permission and whether likeness/voice consent is needed.
- **Acceptance:** observable checks and a fallback if the result fails.

Avoid reproducing a long upstream screenplay or solving conflicting directions with a longer negative-prompt list. Complex scenes often need separate shots and editorial construction. Use reference sheets to aid continuity, not as a guarantee.

## Hybrid workflow

1. Approve storyboard and rough timing using inexpensive original placeholders clearly labeled as such.
2. Generate or capture only the needed plates under the approved scope. Preserve originals and metadata; never present a substitute as a successful generation.
3. Select by anatomy, object permanence, direction, framing and edit compatibility. Log rejected takes and why. Do not retry without limit.
4. Normalize dimensions, frame rate, alpha and color assumptions in a separate working copy. Keep a manifest linking each file to its original.
5. Composite deterministic overlays: real text, licensed logos, sourced numbers, masks and captions. Do not ask the image/video model to reproduce evidence text.
6. Resolve mismatched identity or motion with a different take or an honest cut, not blur that conceals a misleading result.
7. Mix approved audio and inspect the final encoded film end to end.

## Validation and limits

Check shot transitions, hands and interactions, consistent screen direction, reflections, legible overlays, sound perspective and any visible product claim. Compare references and output without assuming that a successful API response means a usable take. Store model/version, settings, source hashes, request IDs, reported cost and accepted/rejected status where available, without secrets.

A seed is not a guarantee that a hosted model will reproduce pixels. Archive accepted media. Generated content may still raise copyright, trademark, publicity or privacy issues. This guide does not establish rights to any provider output and does not include or test a provider adapter.
